# Scouting flagged zones

## How to scout a low-cover zone
Walk to the zone with the map open and look at the plants and the ground, not
just the canopy. Check whether plants are missing (planter skip, poor
emergence, crusting, seed depth), present but small (compaction, cold or wet
soil at planting, herbicide carryover), present but pale or yellow (nutrient
or water issues, nodulation problems), or damaged (insect feeding, hail,
wildlife, disease lesions). Dig a few plants to look at roots and nodules:
healthy nodules are pink inside. Note the pattern of the zone: straight lines
follow equipment (planter rows, sprayer passes, tramlines), rounded patches
follow soil or water, and field edges point at compaction, shading or drift.

## Common causes by pattern
Low, wet areas that stay thin usually mean ponding or poor drainage; look for
crusted soil and root rot. Headlands and turn rows are often compacted by
equipment traffic. Sandy knolls dry out first and show drought stress before
the rest of the field. Oval patches elongated along the tillage direction are
a classic sign of soybean cyst nematode. Yellow patches on high-pH or
calcareous soils early in the season suggest iron deficiency chlorosis.
Regular stripes match planter, sprayer or fertiliser passes and point at an
application or equipment problem rather than the soil.

## What to record
Take a geotagged photo at each spot, note the growth stage, the plant count
per metre of row, and what you saw on leaves, stems and roots. Record the
date so the observation can be lined up with the survey. Scout the same zones
again after the next flight to see whether they are spreading, stable or
recovering; that trend is more informative than any single visit.

## When to get help
If you find lesions, wilting, unusual leaf patterns or insects you cannot
identify, contact your local agricultural extension office or agronomist and
send them the photos; many offer free diagnosis. Any treatment decision (seed,
fertiliser, herbicide, fungicide, insecticide) should be based on a confirmed
cause, the growth stage, thresholds and the product label, not on a drone map
alone. AgroTwin deliberately does not recommend products.
